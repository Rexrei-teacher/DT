Types of Motion 10 questions

Study Guide

Motion TypeDescriptionExample
LinearMovement in a straight line, in one direction.A drawer sliding open; a lift moving up a shaft.
RotaryMovement in a full circle around a fixed centre point.A wheel turning; a spinning drill bit.
ReciprocatingStraight-line movement that repeats back and forth.A piston in an engine; a sewing machine needle.
OscillatingSwinging movement back and forth around a pivot, through an arc.A pendulum; a see-saw; a rocking chair.

Key distinction: reciprocating motion travels along a straight line, while oscillating motion swings through an arc around a fixed pivot. Both are repetitive and change direction, but the path they follow is different.

Which mechanism converts which motion?

MechanismConverts
Cam and followerRotary → Reciprocating or Oscillating
Crank and sliderRotary ↔ Reciprocating
Bell crank / wiper linkageReciprocating → Oscillating (also changes direction)
Gears / pulleysRotary → Rotary (changes speed, torque or direction)
Mechanisms 10 questions

Study Guide

Levers — classified by the position of the fulcrum (F), load (L) and effort (E):

ClassOrderExample
Class 1Fulcrum is between the load and the effort.See-saw, scissors, crowbar.
Class 2Load is between the fulcrum and the effort.Wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener.
Class 3Effort is between the fulcrum and the load.Fishing rod, tweezers, human forearm.

Linkages — systems of connected levers that transmit or transform motion:

Linkage TypeFunctionExample
Bell crankChanges direction of motion 90°, can change magnitude.Bicycle brakes; pedal mechanisms.
Reverse motionReverses the direction of the input motion.Scissors; pliers.
Parallel motionKeeps a component moving parallel to itself.Vehicle suspension; folding rulers.
Crank and sliderConverts rotary ↔ reciprocating motion.Car engine pistons.
Bell crank / wiper linkageConverts reciprocating → oscillating motion.Car windscreen wipers.

Cams and Followers — a cam converts rotary motion into reciprocating or oscillating motion:

Cam TypeMotion produced
EccentricSmooth, gradual rise and fall.
Pear-shapedRise, a dwell period, then a fall.
Snail (drop)Gradual constant rise, then a sudden drop.
Heart-shapedConstant velocity rise and fall.

Follower types: knife-edge (precise, wears fast), roller (reduces friction), flat/plate (durable), point (fine tracing).

Gears 10 questions

Study Guide

Velocity Ratio (VR):

VR = teeth on DRIVEN gear ÷ teeth on DRIVER gear

Output speed (W2):

W2 = W1 ÷ VR   (W1 = input/driver speed)

Compound gear trains (two or more stages):

Overall VR = VR(stage 1) × VR(stage 2) × ...

Torque (ideal system, no losses) — power is conserved, so torque × speed stays constant across the train:

T1 × W1 = T2 × W2  →  T2 = T1 × (W1 ÷ W2) = T1 × VR

In other words: whatever factor the VR reduces speed by, it increases torque by the same factor (ideal case). For a multi-stage train, use the overall VR in this formula.

Worked example: Driver = 15 teeth, driven = 45 teeth, input speed = 120 rpm, input torque = 2 Nm.
VR = 45 ÷ 15 = 3  |  W2 = 120 ÷ 3 = 40 rpm  |  T2 = 2 × 3 = 6 Nm
